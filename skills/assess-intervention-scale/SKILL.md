# Assess Intervention Scale

Treat transfer and scale as new empirical questions. A valid result in one setting does not establish effectiveness in another population, channel, time, or level of exposure.

## Define source and target

Document:

- source evidence, population, context, delivery, comparator, outcome, duration, and effect uncertainty;
- target population, behavior, environment, channel, implementer, exposure, and operating constraints;
- what must remain invariant for the proposed mechanism to work;
- planned scale, rollout speed, marginal cost, dependencies, and affected bystanders;
- user welfare, organizational objective, and stop authority.

Classify the source evidence: theory, laboratory result, intention survey, usability test, field experiment, single-site pilot, multi-site replication, or scaled operation. Do not treat these as interchangeable.

## Audit voltage risks

Read [scale-assessment.md](references/scale-assessment.md). Evaluate five failure families:

1. **False positive:** the original effect may be statistical, analytical, publication, or implementation error.
2. **Audience mismatch:** early participants differ in motivation, needs, access, culture, risk, or response.
3. **Unscalable ingredients:** results depend on exceptional people, supervision, novelty, fidelity, or resources.
4. **Spillovers:** wider adoption changes incentives, norms, capacity, competition, substitution, or bystander behavior.
5. **Cost trap:** acquisition, support, fraud, quality control, or delivery costs worsen at the margin.

Identify conflicting consequences: one mechanic can activate several mechanisms with opposing effects. A message that informs may also license, annoy, stigmatize, or reveal an undesirable norm.

## Audit translation and the last yard

Read [transfer-checklist.md](references/transfer-checklist.md). Compare source and target on population, behavior, setting, timing, channel, messenger, friction, incentives, norms, language, devices, implementation, and alternatives.

Inspect the final delivery details in situ. A sound intervention can fail because the prompt is unseen, the channel is distrusted, the action is inaccessible, the local workflow is different, or operational staff cannot deliver the intended treatment.

Assess whether the intervention will feel acceptable, expected, transparent, and non-creepy. More personalization is not automatically better.

## Apply stage gates

Use the smallest next commitment that resolves the largest scale uncertainty:

1. Reanalyze and reproduce the source result.
2. Test mechanism and comprehension in the target context.
3. Run a local randomized pilot with implementation checks.
4. Replicate across meaningful sites or segments.
5. Ramp exposure with real-time safety and quality guardrails.
6. Preserve a holdout or staggered comparison for durability when feasible.
7. Scale only after costs, operations, heterogeneity, and harm are decision-ready.

Use APEASE: affordability, practicability, effectiveness and cost-effectiveness, acceptability, side effects and safety, and equity.

## Set the decision

Return one recommendation:

- scale;
- scale conditionally with adaptations and guardrails;
- replicate before scale;
- redesign and retest;
- stop.

State which finding would reverse the recommendation. Do not hide a high-consequence uncertainty inside an average confidence score.

## Deliver the assessment

Use the user's language and include:

1. Source claim and target deployment
2. Evidence maturity and uncertainty
3. Transfer-distance table
4. Voltage-risk findings
5. Conflicting consequences and spillovers
6. Last-yard and operational constraints
7. APEASE assessment
8. Stage-gated rollout and experiment plan
9. Decision, stop conditions, and evidence required to proceed
