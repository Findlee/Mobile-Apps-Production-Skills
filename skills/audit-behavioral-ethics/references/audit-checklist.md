# Behavioral Ethics Audit Checklist

## 1. Benefit by the person's own lights

- Is the target outcome something the affected person values?
- Is the claim supported by evidence rather than the organization speaking for them?
- Does the intervention improve an outcome beyond engagement, conversion, or revenue?
- Could the same mechanism push behavior past a beneficial level?

## 2. Autonomy and meaningful choice

- Can the person understand that a choice exists?
- Are refusal and alternatives accessible?
- Is consent freely given under the actual power relationship?
- Can the choice be reversed without retaliation or disproportionate loss?

## 3. Transparency and comprehension

- Are purpose, sponsor, material terms, total cost, data use, and consequences visible before commitment?
- Can a representative user explain the choice accurately?
- Are recommendations and personalization explainable?
- Are uncertainty and limitations communicated proportionately?

## 4. Friction symmetry and exit

- Compare steps, time, channels, waiting, and emotional pressure for entry versus exit.
- Can users cancel, refund, delete, withdraw consent, or obtain entitled benefits?
- Is protective friction proportionate to risk?
- Does any friction primarily preserve company revenue or data access?

## 5. Habit and emotional exploitation

- Is repetition necessary for user value?
- Are distress, loneliness, fear, shame, or social rejection used as triggers?
- Are variable rewards, streaks, loss, or scarcity used to prolong time or spending?
- Are there natural stopping cues, quiet hours, pause, and recovery?

## 6. Privacy and personalization

- Is every data field necessary for the user-valued purpose?
- Is sensitive inference required and expected?
- Can personalization create discrimination, vulnerability, or a filter bubble?
- Can the person access, correct, export, and delete relevant data?

## 7. Distribution and vulnerability

- Test effects on children, older adults, low-literacy users, disabled users, financially stressed users, people with addiction risk, and people subject to institutional power.
- Identify who receives benefits and who bears errors or exclusion.
- Avoid averaging away harm concentrated in a smaller group.

## 8. Incentives and governance

- What metrics and compensation encourage the team?
- Who can stop launch or experiment?
- Is there independent review for high-risk work?
- Are complaints, harm signals, and reversals monitored?
- Are stop conditions and an accountable owner defined?
- Do compensation, quotas, rankings, and dashboards reward the same outcome the organization publicly claims to value?
- Could the metric encourage quality loss, short-termism, concealment, or harm shifted to another group?

## Intervention acceptance

- Would a representative person expect the sponsor to use this data, timing, messenger, or level of personalization?
- Does the intervention imply hidden surveillance or knowledge that the person did not knowingly provide?
- Can the sponsor explain why the person received it without embarrassment or euphemism?
- Could a less personalized or less intrusive design retain most of the benefit?

## Experiencing and remembering selves

Evaluate both moment-to-moment experience and retrospective evaluation. Avoid optimizing only a memorable peak or ending while ignoring long periods of burden, and avoid assuming a pleasant interaction proves durable welfare.
