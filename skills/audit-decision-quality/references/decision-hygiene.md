# Decision Hygiene Patterns

## Shared scale

Define each rating point with observable anchors, boundary examples, and explicit relevant factors. Calibrate with common cases. Avoid vague labels such as "strong" or "high quality" without anchors.

## Mediating assessments

For a complex decision:

1. Identify a small set of distinct assessments that causally matter.
2. Define each assessment and its evidence before reviewing cases.
3. Evaluate them independently and record them.
4. Keep assessments as factual or comparative as possible.
5. Form the holistic judgment only after the components are visible.
6. Document how components combine and when an exception is allowed.

Avoid decompositions so long that judges mechanically click through or invent precision.

## Information sequencing

- Show task-relevant evidence before identities, prior verdicts, or outcome information that could contaminate judgment.
- Hide other judges' ratings until an independent estimate is recorded.
- Reveal contextual information in stages when it is needed to answer a defined question.
- Preserve an audit trail of what was known at each stage.

## Independence and aggregation

- Collect estimates before meetings.
- Aggregate only judgments aimed at the same quantity on a comparable scale.
- Use robust summaries when outliers or strategic responses are plausible.
- Weight judges only with preregistered, evidence-based reliability, not status.
- Discuss reasons after the independent signal is preserved.

## Guidelines, rules, and models

Prefer more structure when decisions recur, relevant attributes are stable, similar cases should be treated similarly, and outcomes can validate the rule. Preserve appeal and override paths, then audit whether overrides improve results or merely reintroduce noise.

## Decision observer prompts

- Has an early impression become the story that explains everything else?
- Is one vivid case replacing the relevant base rate?
- Did the first number or senior opinion anchor the group?
- Was disconfirming evidence actively sought?
- Are confidence and evidence quality being confused?
- Would the group reach the same judgment if identities or outcomes were hidden?
