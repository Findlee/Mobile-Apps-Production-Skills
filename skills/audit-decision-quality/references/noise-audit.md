# Noise Audit

## Formal audit design

1. Select representative cases spanning common, borderline, high-stakes, and historically disputed situations.
2. Give the same cases to multiple qualified judges.
3. Standardize instructions, information, scale, and time window.
4. Require independent judgments before any discussion.
5. Capture final judgment, component assessments, confidence, time, and rationale.
6. Compare dispersion, systematic judge severity, attribute weighting, and accuracy when a benchmark exists.
7. Repeat a subset later to estimate within-judge occasion noise when appropriate.

Do not reveal that the purpose is to expose particular people as inconsistent. Audit the system and protect participants from performative blame.

## Limited alternatives

When a prospective audit is unavailable, use:

- duplicate or near-duplicate historical cases;
- inter-rater disagreement and escalation logs;
- overrides, appeals, reversals, and complaint outcomes;
- judge-level severity and approval rates after adjusting for case mix;
- repeated AI evaluations on identical inputs;
- blind re-review of a stratified sample.

Historical comparisons can be confounded by case assignment and missing information. Do not label raw approval-rate differences as noise without checking case mix.
