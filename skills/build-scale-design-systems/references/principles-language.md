# Principles, vocabulary, and system shape

## Principles

A useful principle is specific to the product, memorable, observable in decisions, and able to resolve a genuine tension. Avoid generic virtues such as “simple” without stating what wins when goals conflict.

For each principle define:

| Principle | Tension resolved | Do | Avoid | Product example | System implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Principles should connect product purpose to pattern decisions and evolve when evidence changes.

## Shared vocabulary

Name a pattern by what it helps people do, then define purpose, anatomy, behavior, variants, and relationships. Avoid multiple names for the same thing and appearance-based names that fail after redesign. Maintain aliases and deprecation when renaming widely used concepts.

## Taxonomy

Organize documentation according to users’ mental model: foundations, components, patterns, templates, content, accessibility, platform guidance, or domain areas as appropriate. Test findability with real users; do not mirror the internal team chart by default.

## System shape

Choose intentionally along two dimensions:

- **strict ↔ loose:** how much variation and enforcement is supported;
- **centralized ↔ distributed:** where production, decisions, and contribution live.

A regulated product may need strict foundations; a multi-brand ecosystem may need a shared core with governed extension. The shape can differ across layers.

## Design language

Connect perceptual tokens and expressive rules to desired product character. Document how typography, color, spacing, shape, iconography, imagery, motion, and voice work together; a token inventory without compositional meaning is not a design language.
