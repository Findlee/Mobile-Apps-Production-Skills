# System framing and inventory

## Charter

Define:

- product/customer purpose the system supports;
- internal users and their jobs, stacks, and constraints;
- included products, platforms, brands, and locales;
- problems to solve and outcomes to improve;
- what is explicitly out of scope;
- owner, partners, funding, support, and decision rights;
- first pilot and review horizon.

The system is the connected patterns and shared practices. Code packages, design libraries, documentation, and token files are implementations of that system.

## Inventory method

Sample real production experiences across important products and states. Capture screenshots, code/component identity, design asset, content, behavior, accessibility, responsive/platform variants, usage frequency, defect/support data, owner, and dependencies.

Group:

- **functional patterns** by purpose and behavior: selecting, navigating, confirming, comparing, editing;
- **perceptual patterns** by visual language: type, color, spacing, shape, iconography, motion, voice.

Record whether variation is duplication, product-specific intent, platform convention, accessibility need, experiment, legacy debt, or unknown.

## Candidate score

Score recurrence, user/product impact, current inconsistency cost, accessibility/safety risk, cross-team demand, pilot relevance, technical feasibility, and owner readiness. High recurrence alone does not make a poor pattern worthy of standardization.

## Evidence package

For every promoted candidate retain examples, contexts, rejected variations, known exceptions, and participating teams. This prevents an abstraction from losing the reasons behind it.

## Book foundations

Alla Kholmatova’s *Design Systems* informs the interconnected-pattern, purpose, functional/perceptual, and inventory framing. Dan Mall’s *Design That Scales* informs product-linked, organizational, and adoption framing.
