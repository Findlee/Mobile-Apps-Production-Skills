# Customer Research Source Map

This skill is a synthesis of the five source books in the Product Management Skills package. It is not a chapter summary. The sources below explain which concepts were used to design the runtime behavior.

## Teresa Torres - Continuous Discovery Habits

Primary source for the generative / continuous customer-learning system.

Key concepts incorporated:

- Experience maps as evolving representations of what the team currently thinks it knows about customer context.
- Research questions are different from interview questions.
- Story-based interviews anchored in a specific recent event.
- Temporal excavation of customer stories.
- Returning from generalizations to the specific instance.
- Letting the participant's relevant experience guide the conversation rather than forcing a rigid discussion guide.
- Interview snapshots as one-interview synthesis artifacts.
- Snapshot components: contextual quick facts, memorable quote, insights, opportunities, and a visual representation of the participant's story.
- Opportunities framed as customer needs, pain points, and desires rather than feature requests.
- Continuous / weekly interviewing as a habit for active discovery teams.
- Automating recruiting logistics to make continuous customer exposure sustainable.
- Recruiting through in-product triggers and customer-facing teams.
- Product trio members interviewing together so customer knowledge is not monopolized by one role.
- Continuous synthesis rather than waiting for a large research batch.
- Opportunity extraction and later opportunity-space mapping.

Most relevant chapters:

- Chapter 4 - Visualizing What You Know
- Chapter 5 - Continuous Interviewing
- Chapter 6 - Mapping the Opportunity Space

## Melissa Perri - Escaping the Build Trap

Primary source for stage-appropriate research and root-problem discipline.

Key concepts incorporated:

- Problem exploration and solution exploration are different phases.
- Generative research is used to understand the customer problem and its context.
- Evaluative research, such as usability testing, evaluates a solution rather than discovering whether the underlying problem is important.
- Product teams should avoid defining the problem as the absence of a feature.
- Feature requests should be unpacked to understand what the customer is trying to accomplish.
- Research may reveal that the team's initial problem framing was wrong.
- Research effort should answer the next uncertainty rather than applying a method ritualistically.

Most relevant chapters:

- Chapter 15 - The Product Kata
- Chapter 17 - Problem Exploration
- Chapter 18 - Solution Exploration

## Dan Olsen - The Lean Product Playbook

Primary source for practical user-test planning, recruiting, moderation, and synthesis.

Key concepts incorporated:

- One-on-one sessions reduce group-dynamics problems compared with focus groups.
- Small iterative waves of user testing can expose major usability/value patterns without requiring large research projects.
- In-person, remote moderated, and remote unmoderated research have different strengths and trade-offs.
- Recruit participants from the target market using relevant behavioral and contextual screening criteria.
- Routine scheduling helps avoid the research scheduling trap.
- Contextual observation in the customer's real environment can reveal things interviews miss.
- Research sessions benefit from a prepared but lightweight structure.
- Start with discovery about the customer's current behavior and existing solution before presenting the product.
- Avoid leading questions and answer suggestions.
- Use open-ended questions and minimal moderator intervention.
- Observe how users act rather than coaching them through the task.
- Synthesize functionality, UX, and messaging evidence and compare patterns across participants.
- Small-sample ratings are supporting signals, not population statistics.

Most relevant chapters:

- Chapter 2 - Problem Space versus Solution Space
- Chapter 9 - Test Your MVP with Customers

## Marty Cagan - Inspired

Primary source for direct customer exposure, research-tool limitations, and product validation.

Key concepts incorporated:

- Product managers need direct interaction with users; filtered reports are useful but not a substitute for deep customer understanding.
- Charter / reference customer relationships can provide ongoing access to target users, but the product should not become a custom solution for a few accounts.
- Customer surveys, analytics, field visits, personas, usability testing, and competitive analysis are inputs with different capabilities and limitations.
- Market research should not be treated as a machine that tells the team what product to build.
- Site / field visits can reveal information unavailable through other methods.
- Usability testing should happen early and observe actions, not only words.
- Product validation separates feasibility, usability, and value.
- Prototype testing should minimize moderator leading and observe whether users can perform key tasks and whether they care about the product.
- Testing with real target users before expensive implementation reduces risk.

Most relevant chapters:

- Chapter 15 - Charter User Programs
- Chapter 16 - Market Research
- Chapter 21 - Product Validation
- Chapter 22 - Prototype Testing

## Jeff Patton - User Story Mapping

Primary source for shared understanding, externalizing customer stories, and outcome-oriented context.

Key concepts incorporated:

- Shared documents are not the same as shared understanding.
- Talking, sketching, mapping, and externalizing different perspectives helps a team build shared understanding.
- Product conversations should include who, what, and why, not only the requested functionality.
- Start from the world as it is now and the customer's current behavior before imagining the future solution.
- Research artifacts should help people remember and discuss customer stories rather than pretend to encode every detail perfectly.
- Good personas and product models should be grounded in observed / researched data rather than guesses.
- Observe realistic work and plan to learn from real product use after release.

Most relevant sections:

- Building Shared Understanding
- Now and Later
- Discovery / learning discussions
- Release learning and observation

## Synthesis choices

The skill intentionally does not copy every method literally.

Notable design choices:

- Weekly interviewing is treated as a strong operating default for active continuous discovery, not a mandatory ritual for every product at all times.
- Fixed sample-size rules are not universalized. Olsen's small-wave guidance is useful for iterative evaluative testing, while continuous generative research follows a different operating model.
- Think-aloud is not mandated. The skill prefers observable behavior and neutral follow-up because the source books differ in moderation style, and task realism may be harmed by forcing continuous narration.
- Personas are not a required output. When used, they must be grounded in real evidence rather than fictional detail.
- Surveys are treated as one evidence source, not as automatic product prioritization or proof of future behavior.
- Research findings end in a product decision implication or explicit next uncertainty rather than a standalone theme report.
