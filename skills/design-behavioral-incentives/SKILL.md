# Design Behavioral Incentives

Make the behavior encouraged by the incentive match the outcome and values the system claims to support. Treat an incentive as both a payoff and a signal about what the sponsor truly values.

## Define the behavioral contract

Identify:

- actor, sponsor, payer, beneficiary, and affected bystanders;
- observable target behavior and user-valued outcome;
- current behavior and diagnosed barrier;
- stated message, formal metric, reward or penalty, timing, and eligibility;
- what a rational actor would do to maximize the incentive;
- behavior that is valuable but unmeasured;
- who bears errors, risk, cost, and delayed consequences.

Do not use an incentive to compensate for missing ability, access, trust, or opportunity unless the incentive directly removes that barrier.

## Audit the signals

Read [incentive-audit.md](references/incentive-audit.md). Compare what the organization says with what its rewards, prices, targets, and reactions to failure communicate.

Check at least these conflicts:

1. Quantity versus quality
2. Short-term output versus long-term outcome
3. Individual reward versus teamwork
4. Innovation versus punishment of good-faith failure
5. Business value versus user welfare
6. Extrinsic reward versus identity, norms, or intrinsic motive
7. Measured activity versus unmeasured substitution or gaming

State the social signal and self-signal created by participating. An otherwise attractive reward may imply that the action is unpleasant, low-status, transactional, or done for the wrong reason.

## Choose whether to incentivize

Prefer, in order:

1. Remove the actual barrier.
2. Align defaults, tools, information, and workflow with the desired outcome.
3. Provide timely feedback or recognition.
4. Use an incentive only when motivation or opportunity is plausibly limiting and the target can be measured without severe distortion.

Use incentives diagnostically when useful: a response to a temporary incentive may reveal that ability exists and willingness or opportunity is limiting. Do not assume the effect will persist after removal.

## Design the incentive

Specify:

- exact eligible behavior or outcome;
- reward currency, size, timing, certainty, duration, and funding;
- quality, welfare, equity, and anti-gaming constraints;
- how teamwork and delayed outcomes are represented;
- what happens on partial success, failure, reversal, and exit;
- whether the incentive is visible, explainable, and perceived as fair;
- how it ends without creating an entitlement shock or destroying the routine.

Use the smallest effective incentive. Avoid simultaneously changing size, timing, framing, and eligibility when the goal is to learn which mechanism matters.

## Test second-order effects

Predict both the intended pathway and at least two plausible counter-pathways. Check for:

- quality erosion, risk shifting, metric gaming, fraud, or concealment;
- crowd-out of intrinsic or prosocial motivation;
- selection of the wrong participants;
- inequitable access or disproportionate penalties;
- reference-point, loss, regret, or sunk-cost pressure;
- behavior that stops when payment stops;
- spillovers to users, coworkers, suppliers, or future periods.

Route high-stakes penalties, loss framing, children, health, employment, finance, addiction, or essential services through `$audit-behavioral-ethics` when available.

## Validate and scale

Test the real outcome, not only the rewarded action. Measure quality, substitution, persistence after removal, distributional effects, complaints, and gaming. Use `$design-behavior-experiments` for causal testing and `$assess-intervention-scale` before broad rollout when available.

## Deliver the specification

Use the user's language and include:

1. Behavioral contract and stakeholder map
2. Current incentive and signal audit
3. Mixed-signal and gaming risks
4. Recommended incentive or non-incentive alternative
5. Exact rules, timing, and eligibility
6. Intended and countervailing mechanisms
7. Ethics and fairness safeguards
8. Measurement, removal, and scale plan
