# Incentive and Signal Audit

## Behavioral contract


## Mixed-signal matrix

| Stated priority | Common conflicting incentive | Likely response | Safer design question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality | Pay only for volume or speed | Rush, omit, lower standards | Can quality be constrained, sampled, or rewarded? |
| Long-term value | Reward this week's output | Pull value forward, defer harm | Can delayed outcomes or retention of quality enter the rule? |
| Teamwork | Rank and reward individuals | Hoard leads, information, or credit | Can shared outcomes and individual contribution both matter? |
| Innovation | Punish every failed attempt | Hide failure, avoid experiments | Can good process and learning be separated from negligence? |
| User welfare | Pay for engagement or spend | Overuse, pressure, unsuitable sales | Can compensation depend on durable user outcomes and guardrails? |
| Honesty | Reward only favorable results | Conceal errors and adverse evidence | Is reporting bad news safe and valued? |

## Design checks

- **Target:** Reward the outcome when measurable; otherwise constrain the proxy's failure modes.
- **Currency:** Match money, time, access, recognition, feedback, or contribution to the actor and context.
- **Timing:** Make the contingency understandable without creating myopic optimization.
- **Magnitude:** Avoid amounts so small they insult or reframe a prosocial act, or so large they coerce.
- **Frame:** Loss and regret can be powerful but impose stress and reference-point pressure; require strong welfare justification.
- **Fairness:** Account for unequal opportunity, resources, starting position, and exposure to the target behavior.
- **Identity:** Ask what accepting the incentive lets the person infer about themselves.
- **Social signal:** Ask what participation communicates to peers, customers, or the community.
- **Exit:** Define duration, renewal, earned-value protection, and the post-incentive state.

## Counterfactual questions

1. What would people do if they cared only about the metric?
2. Which important dimension would deteriorate first?
3. Who can meet the target most easily for reasons unrelated to effort or value?
4. What behavior moves to another channel, person, or time period?
5. What will people hide if failure is punished?
6. What happens when the incentive disappears?
7. Could a smaller nonmonetary change remove the real barrier?
