# Habit Safety and Product Guardrails

## Healthy-loop requirements

- The repeated behavior advances a user-valued outcome.
- Each cycle delivers recognizable value.
- Prompts are expected, controllable, and easy to reduce.
- Pausing does not create disproportionate loss.
- Return after interruption is welcoming rather than punitive.
- Exit, deletion, and cancellation are clear.
- The product measures real-world benefit and harm.

## High-risk patterns

Escalate to an ethics audit when the design includes:

- infinite feeds or autoplay without a natural stopping cue;
- unpredictable rewards optimized for session length or spend;
- streak loss that creates anxiety, shame, or sunk-cost pressure;
- notifications triggered by loneliness, fear, or distress for unrelated commercial goals;
- social comparison tied to identity or public embarrassment;
- spending loops, loot boxes, gambling-like mechanics, or children;
- deliberate sleep interruption;
- synthetic scarcity or expiring value that the user earned;
- difficult pause, cancellation, or data deletion.

## Safer alternatives

- Add natural stopping points and session summaries.
- Let users choose cadence, quiet hours, and reminder type.
- Preserve streak history while allowing rest, recovery, or grace periods.
- Replace uncertain extrinsic rewards with progress, mastery, completion, or meaningful content.
- Show time spent and support user-set limits.
- Use finite sessions when the user goal is finite.
- Separate recovery from reactivation marketing.

## Metrics

### User outcome

Measure the real-world benefit the routine is meant to create.

### Loop health

Track time to first value, successful repetitions, prompt dependence, voluntary return, and recovery after interruption.

### Harm guardrails

Track regret, unwanted use, notification disablement, sleep disruption, overspending, complaints, support contacts, failed cancellation, and effects on vulnerable segments.

High engagement with worsening user outcomes is failure.
