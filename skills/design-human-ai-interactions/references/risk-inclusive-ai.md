# Risk and inclusive AI review

## Risk map

| Harm | Affected actor | Scenario | Likelihood | Severity | Detectability | Prevention | Recourse | Owner |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|

Consider allocation/denial, stereotyping, harassment, misinformation, privacy/inference, security/abuse, financial or physical harm, exclusion by language/ability, labor displacement, loss of agency, reputational harm, and effects on people who never used the interface.

## Inclusion

- Identify who is absent or underrepresented in training, evaluation, research, and product teams.
- Co-design with people who experience likely failure; do not infer needs from demographic labels alone.
- Test languages, dialects, assistive technology, literacy, devices, connectivity, and culturally different task strategies.
- Analyze intersections and small slices where averages hide severe failure.
- Provide an equivalent non-AI or human route where exclusion from AI blocks an essential outcome.

## Data and feedback

Review provenance, consent, purpose, minimization, retention, sensitive inference, annotation labor, representativeness, and downstream reuse. Qualitative “thick” evidence can reveal harms that aggregate labels and metrics miss.

## Recourse

People affected by a consequential output need notice appropriate to the context, an understandable reason, a way to correct data or context, meaningful human review, timely appeal, and remediation. The reviewing human needs authority and information, not a ceremonial approval step.
