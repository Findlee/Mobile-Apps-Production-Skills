# Inclusive process

## Exclusion map

For each essential activity, record:

| Activity or decision | Mismatch | Who may be excluded | Context | Consequence | Evidence | Partner needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Look across perception, cognition, communication, dexterity, mobility, speech, hearing, vision, literacy, language, connectivity, device access, privacy, and time pressure. These are lenses, not fixed user categories.

## Participation model

- Recruit people with lived experience of the relevant mismatch early enough to shape the problem.
- Pay and credit participants appropriately; make participation itself accessible.
- Include affected non-users and support people where the product can affect them.
- Give partners real influence over priorities and interpretation, not only prototype feedback.
- Do not treat one participant as representative of an entire community.
- Record conflicts between needs instead of forcing false consensus.

## Persona spectrum

Use one human activity to examine related permanent, temporary, and situational constraints. The spectrum should reveal possible adaptations, not imply that experiences are equivalent. A solution developed with a specific excluded group may extend to other contexts; validate the extension.

## Research safeguards

- Avoid disability simulation as evidence of lived experience.
- Ask how people currently accomplish the task and what workarounds cost them.
- Separate barriers created by the product from preferences about the product.
- Protect sensitive identity and health information.
- State whose perspective is missing from the evidence.

## Decision test

A proposed solution is stronger when it removes a mismatch, preserves autonomy, supports an equivalent outcome, works with common assistive strategies, and does not create a new burden for another group.
