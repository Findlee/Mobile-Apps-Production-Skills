# Consent and choice architecture

## Choice quality

A choice should be informed, specific enough for its purposes, freely made, unambiguous where required, and as easy to revisit or withdraw as the context requires. Confirm the exact legal interpretation with current counsel.

## Screen structure

1. State the immediate feature or decision.
2. Explain the requested data/commitment and important consequence.
3. Present clear accept and decline/later choices with honest visual weight.
4. Offer granular detail where purposes differ.
5. Link the enduring policy or settings without hiding the essential facts there.

Compare independent alternatives on the same decision level: screens and taps, target size, contrast, reading complexity, interruption, and prominence. A material asymmetry needs a user-serving reason, not a conversion target.

## Defaults and bundling

- Do not preselect optional consent when active choice is required.
- Do not bundle unrelated purposes or make optional marketing a condition of a core service.
- Avoid a single “agree” covering data use, terms, subscription, and marketing when these require distinct understanding.
- Preserve prior refusal; ask again only after a meaningful context or purpose change.

## Timing

Ask at the point of value and intent, not automatically at launch. For OS permissions, explain before the system prompt only when the user can proceed directly. For new purposes, ask before the new use begins.

## After the choice

Provide a receipt or clear current state, settings entry, change/withdrawal path, effect timing, and confirmation. Explain what historical processing cannot be reversed without using that fact to discourage withdrawal.

For trials and recurring commitments, show the price, billing interval, first charge date, renewal behavior, cancellation path, and benefit end date before commitment. Determine whether current law or store policy requires a pre-renewal reminder and implement it; do not claim one universal reminder rule.

## Copy test

A person should be able to say what they accepted, for what purpose, what refusal changes, and how to change it later. If they cannot, simplify the decision rather than adding more legal text.
