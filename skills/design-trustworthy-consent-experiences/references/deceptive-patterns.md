# Deceptive-pattern red-team

Review the full journey, not one screenshot.

## Visual and wording manipulation

- acceptance is emphasized while refusal is hidden, low contrast, ambiguous, or split across screens;
- decline uses shame, fear, or a double negative;
- material cost, renewal, data use, or consequence is visually separated from the action;
- a control looks like neutral navigation or content but commits the user;
- false scarcity, urgency, popularity, or countdown pressure is used.

## Obstruction and persistence

- joining or accepting is much easier than cancellation, deletion, or withdrawal;
- repeated prompts ignore a recent refusal;
- a required route is buried in unrelated settings or support loops;
- progress is lost when declining an optional choice;
- the interface adds unnecessary authentication or contact steps only to exit.

## Defaults and hidden effects

- optional items, tips, insurance, subscriptions, tracking, or sharing are preselected without a sound basis;
- a free or trial action silently becomes recurring payment;
- an action has an undisclosed secondary purpose;
- privacy settings reset or become broader after an update;
- a “save” or close action interprets silence as acceptance.

## Fairness comparison

Compare accept and decline for number of steps, time, reading complexity, prominence, target size, interruptions, emotional framing, and access to the core service. Difference must be justified by the real task, not conversion goals.

## Response

For each finding name the mechanism, evidence, affected decision, user harm, recommended neutral alternative, and current regulator/platform source that may apply.
