# Privacy framing

## Purpose and necessity table

| Data/action | User-facing purpose | Essential? | Source | Recipients | Retention | Consequence if declined | Less-invasive option | Owner |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Ask whether the product can deliver the goal with less data, lower precision, on-device processing, shorter retention, later collection, or an existing system picker rather than broad access.

## Actors and lifecycle

Map the person making the choice, account owner, people represented in the data, product operator, processors/partners, recipients, automated systems, and future users. Follow data from request and collection through inference, use, sharing, retention, export, deletion, backup, and incident handling.

## User questions the experience must answer

- What is requested or committed?
- Why is it needed now and which feature benefits?
- Is it required or optional?
- Who receives or can see it?
- How long does it persist and is new information inferred?
- What happens if I say no?
- How can I review, change, withdraw, export, or delete it?

## Escalation

Request current legal/privacy review for jurisdiction, lawful basis, age/guardian issues, sensitive categories, cross-border transfer, profiling, biometrics, children, financial/health use, data-subject rights, breach obligations, and retention. Product text must not invent a legal basis.

Assess combinations, not only individual fields: location plus health, identity plus behavior, or contact graph plus activity can create a more sensitive inference and threat model than either datum alone. Record abuse cases, access controls, notification exposure, on-device versus server processing, and incident response.
