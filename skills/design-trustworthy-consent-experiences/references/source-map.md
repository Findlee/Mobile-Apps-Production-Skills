# Current privacy and deceptive-design sources

Use current jurisdiction and platform sources for every formal review. These references provide frameworks, not universal legal conclusions.

- [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework) — organizational privacy risk management and communication.
- [FTC: Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light) — US regulator analysis of deceptive interface practices.
- [EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 on deceptive design patterns](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-032022-on-deceptive-design-patterns-in-social-media-platform_en) — European data-protection guidance.
- [European Commission: data-protection information for individuals](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-individuals_en) — overview of GDPR rights and consent conditions; verify exact law and guidance for the use case.
- [Apple privacy guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy) — current Apple experience expectations.
- [Android permission guidance](https://developer.android.com/training/permissions/requesting) — current Android runtime-permission behavior.

## Source discipline

Record jurisdiction, platform/store, policy/version, publication or access date, and reviewer. Escalate conflicts and uncertainty to qualified legal/privacy owners. Do not turn general design advice into a statement that processing is lawful.
