# Evidence Guide for Barrier Diagnosis

## Ask about episodes, not opinions

Prefer: "Tell me about the last time you tried to do this. What happened immediately before and after?"

Avoid relying on: "Would you use this?", "Why don't you care?", or abstract explanations of personality.

## Collect evidence by question

| Diagnostic question | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| Did the cue arrive? | Delivery, exposure, visibility, recall, notification settings |
| What was the immediate reaction? | Observation, think-aloud, recent-episode interview, language used |
| How was value evaluated? | Comparison behavior, pricing comprehension, trade-off interview |
| Could the actor act? | Step timing, error logs, accessibility test, permissions, resource inventory |
| Was the moment viable? | Contextual inquiry, time-of-day data, competing events, deferral pattern |
| Did experience suppress action? | Support history, prior failures, trust research, cohort differences |

## Separate evidence types

- **Fact:** directly observed or measured
- **Report:** actor's account of an episode
- **Inference:** explanation consistent with evidence
- **Assumption:** unverified premise needed to proceed
- **Unknown:** material gap

## Make hypotheses falsifiable

Weak: "Users suffer from loss aversion."

Strong: "Because the interface frames setup as surrendering account control, first-time users abandon before authorization; reframing the same permissions as reversible controls should improve authorization without increasing regret."

## Look for disconfirming cases

Compare:

- completers versus non-completers;
- first-time versus experienced actors;
- successful contexts versus failed contexts;
- actors exposed to the cue versus not exposed;
- people who found an alternative route;
- different accessibility, language, or device conditions.

## Avoid common errors

- Do not ask only people who completed the journey.
- Do not confuse stated intention with ability in context.
- Do not infer causality from a funnel drop alone.
- Do not average segments with different barriers.
- Do not treat support complaints as prevalence estimates.
- Do not prescribe an intervention before identifying the step and mechanism.
