# Discovery Conversations

## Opportunity

- Who has the problem, and who benefits from solving it?
- What triggers the need?
- What do people do today, including non-software workarounds?
- What evidence shows the problem is frequent or costly?
- What would change if the problem were solved?

## Users and context

- Which user types have materially different goals or constraints?
- Where, when, and on which devices does the work happen?
- What knowledge, permissions, accessibility needs, or connectivity constraints matter?
- Who approves, supports, receives, or corrects the result?

## Outcome and measurement

- What user behavior should become easier, faster, safer, or more successful?
- Which user signal changes before the business metric?
- What baseline exists?
- What result would falsify the proposed solution?

## Solution exploration

- What is the smallest artifact that can test the key assumption?
- Which alternatives solve the same problem with less software?
- What could users misunderstand or misuse?
- Which part is valuable, usable, and feasible but still uncertain?

## Conversation discipline

Use words, sketches, examples, and maps together. Record artifacts to support memory, then retell the reasoning. Do not treat agreement with a document as proof of shared understanding.

Conceptual lineage: shared understanding, product discovery, and validated learning.
