# Slicing Heuristics

## Slice an experiment

Choose one risky belief and define:

- the evidence needed;
- the smallest credible test;
- the decision the evidence will inform;
- the stopping rule.

Avoid production architecture when a prototype, interview, fake door, concierge service, or technical spike can answer the question.

## Slice a release

Start with one user, one goal, and the shortest coherent journey. Reduce scope by limiting:

- roles or customer segments;
- input/data variants;
- channels or platforms;
- integrations;
- automation level;
- policy variants;
- scale or geography;
- advanced editing, reporting, and customization.

Do not reduce essential security, data integrity, accessibility, or recovery below responsible use.

## Slice development

Create a walking skeleton through real boundaries, then add behavior. Useful sequences include:

1. happy path;
2. persistence and resumption;
3. validation and user correction;
4. dependency failure and recovery;
5. scale, performance, and polish.

## Test slice coherence

- Can a user achieve an outcome without a later slice?
- Can the team demonstrate it end to end?
- Does it reduce uncertainty or deliver value?
- Is the omitted work explicit?
- Is it smaller because of narrower scope rather than hidden quality debt?

Conceptual lineage: MVP release slicing, learning loops, and incremental development.
