## 1. Dimensions

Assess sponsorship, technology role, competence, durability/ownership/access, strategy/problem selection, discovery, delivery/monitor/support/rollback, trust/coaching, stakeholders/commitments/rights, staffing/funding/dependencies.

## 2. Evidence

Use cross-role interviews, artifacts, meeting observation, roadmap/backlog samples, recent discovery/release/incident cases, analytics, and direct observation. Ask who chose the last problem/solution, what alternatives were rejected, what evidence changed a decision, when engineering observed a customer, how a failed release was handled, and why a commitment could not change.

## 3. Findings

Report observed majority pattern, positive exceptions, competence versus structural constraint, outcome/learning consequence, smallest demonstrable change, and required authority/sponsorship. Do not use unexplained maturity scores or judge individuals from process artifacts.

## 4. Pilot

Choose willing participants, credible leaders, capable product/design/engineering coverage, meaningful work, measurable outcome, manageable dependencies/commitments, and supportive stakeholders. Define baseline, outcome, rights, coaching, review points, and expand/stop criteria. Prefer deep bounded pilots.

## 5. Risks

Watch for sponsorship without changed decisions, titles without competence, autonomy without strategy/access/coaching, outcome accountability without authority/data, detached innovation labs, discovery as polish/refinement, training/artifact adoption as transformation metrics, commitments consuming capacity, stakeholders losing control before competence earns trust, and scaling before evidence.
