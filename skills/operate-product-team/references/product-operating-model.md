## 1. Team patterns

| Pattern | Receives | Accountable for | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery team | Backlog tasks | Completion | Little user access or decisions |
| Feature team | Roadmap solutions | Feature delivery | Stakeholders prescribe solutions |
| Product team | Problems, outcomes, constraints | Customer/business result | Discovers and delivers solutions |

Use observed behavior, not names.

## 2. Competencies

- Product: value, viability, data, customer, market, business.
- Design: complete experience, usability, interaction, user insight.
- Engineering: feasibility, enabling technology, delivery, security, reliability, operations.
- Leadership: vision, strategy, topology, coaching, staffing, obstacle removal.

Add qualified domain ownership when risk exceeds core competence.

## 3. Principles

Durable cross-functional teams; problems rather than prescribed features; outcomes over output; discovery-delivery ownership; early collaboration; focused strategy; responsible risk tests; small instrumented releases; judgment over ritual; trust/learning over predictability theater.

## 4. Decision rights

| Decision | Recommends | Decides | Must consult | Escalation condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Defer to relevant expertise. Use evidence/tests when possible. Record material dissent and review triggers. No team is empowered if every meaningful decision needs upstream solution approval.

## 5. Leaders and stakeholders

Leaders select focused problems, build competence, provide context/access/authority, remove structural obstacles, and evaluate outcome/learning. Stakeholders provide expertise, constraints, commitments, and consequences; challenge evidence and viability; and avoid shadow feature roadmaps when teams own outcomes.
