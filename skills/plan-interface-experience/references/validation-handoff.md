# Validation and handoff

Use this reference to turn uncertainty into a practical next step.

## Evidence table

Classify important statements:

| Type | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | Supported by supplied or cited evidence | Preserve source and scope |
| Assumption | Working belief needed to proceed | State confidence and consequence |
| Hypothesis | Testable relationship or expected behavior | Define evidence that would support or refute it |
| Constraint | Boundary imposed by context, policy, technology, or strategy | Confirm owner and flexibility |
| Decision | Chosen direction among alternatives | Record rationale and reconsideration condition |

Do not label stakeholder opinion or generated content as fact.

## Prioritize uncertainty

Test assumptions with the largest combination of:

- consequence if wrong;
- uncertainty;
- difficulty of changing later;
- influence on other decisions.

Avoid spending research effort on low-impact details that can be changed cheaply.

## Choose validation method

- **Context interview or observation:** understand activities, workarounds, language, and environment.
- **Concept test:** check whether the value and model make sense before detailed interaction.
- **Card sorting or tree test:** investigate grouping, labels, and findability.
- **Prototype usability test:** observe discovery, execution, interpretation, and recovery.
- **Technical spike:** test platform, performance, data, or integration feasibility.
- **Content test:** verify labels, instructions, comprehension, or trust.
- **Instrumented release or experiment:** measure behavior only after ethical, interpretable success criteria exist.

AI may prepare or analyze these methods but must not fabricate participants or observations.

## Handoff package

Include only what the next phase needs:

- problem and outcome;
- relevant users and context;
- activity and object model;
- primary and alternative flows;
- information architecture;
- screen and state inventory;
- permissions and high-consequence points;
- assumptions, risks, and validation plan;
- explicit non-goals.

## Decision checkpoints

Before pattern selection, confirm the flow and object model. Before visual design, confirm structural patterns and content priority. Before implementation, confirm state ownership, recovery guarantees, and acceptance criteria. Before release, audit the implementation and observe representative users on critical tasks.

## Success criteria

Define both:

- **effectiveness:** users can achieve the intended real-world outcome accurately;
- **experience quality:** they can understand, control, recover, and trust the process.

Add business metrics without allowing them to justify coercion, hidden costs, exclusion, or user harm.
