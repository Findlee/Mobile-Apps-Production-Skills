# Product Strategy Source Map

This skill is a synthesis of the uploaded books. The source books are design-time references and are not required at runtime.

## Melissa Perri - Escaping the Build Trap

Primary contributions:

- Strategy as a decision-enabling framework rather than a detailed plan or feature list.
- Knowledge, alignment, and effects gaps as reasons to communicate intent/outcomes rather than prescribe all actions.
- Strategy deployment levels: vision, strategic intent, product initiatives, options.
- Strategy creation as movement from current state toward vision by identifying and addressing obstacles.
- Strategic intents as a small set of high-level, business-focused areas of concentration.
- Product initiatives as product/customer problems that translate business direction into product work.
- Options as possible solution bets rather than fixed strategy.
- Product vision as the coherent customer value/problem frame that should not be overly feature-specific.
- Portfolio questions about how products create value as a system, unique value, guidelines, and what to stop doing.
- Product Kata: direction, current state, next goal, problem/solution exploration, learning.
- Product metrics as a system of leading/lagging metrics rather than output/vanity metrics.

Relevant sections reviewed:

- Part III, Chapters 10-14: strategy, strategic gaps, strategic framework, vision/intents, product vision/portfolio.
- Part IV, Chapters 15-17: Product Kata, success metrics, problem exploration.

## Dan Olsen - The Lean Product Playbook

Primary contributions:

- Product-Market Fit Pyramid: target customer, underserved needs, value proposition, feature set, UX.
- Target-customer segmentation using demographic, psychographic, behavioral, and needs-based dimensions.
- Distinguishing users, buyers, economic buyers, and blockers in B2B contexts.
- Target market as a hypothesis that may change after actual product/customer evidence.
- Customer needs/benefits written from the customer's perspective rather than as features.
- Needs as hypotheses to validate through customer research.
- Customer benefit ladders and the importance of root motivations.
- Hierarchies of needs and prerequisite needs.
- Importance vs satisfaction as a way to reason about underserved opportunities and customer value.
- Value proposition as explicit choice of needs to serve and needs not to serve.
- Must-haves, performance benefits, delighters, competitor/alternative comparison, and differentiation.
- Strategy as saying no and concentrating on dimensions that matter to the target market.
- Forward-looking value proposition that anticipates market/competitor change.

Relevant sections reviewed:

- Chapter 3: Determine Your Target Customer.
- Chapter 4: Identify Underserved Customer Needs.
- Chapter 5: Define Your Value Proposition.

## Marty Cagan - Inspired

Primary contributions:

- Lightweight Product Opportunity Assessment before expensive product commitment.
- Opportunity assessment should define the problem/opportunity rather than prematurely define the solution.
- Questions covering problem, target market, size/importance, success, alternatives, differentiator, why now, go-to-market, critical success factors, and recommendation.
- Product discovery as distinct from execution: first discover a valuable, usable, feasible product, then build it well.
- Product principles as public/durable beliefs that help evaluate alternatives and speed trade-off decisions.
- Product principles should be prioritized, specific enough to guide decisions, and distinct from generic design principles.

Relevant sections reviewed:

- Chapter 11: Assessing Product Opportunities.
- Chapter 12: Product Discovery.
- Chapter 13: Product Principles.

## Teresa Torres - Continuous Discovery Habits

Primary contributions:

- Outcome orientation: define success by customer/business impact rather than shipped output.
- Business outcomes vs product outcomes vs traction metrics.
- Product outcomes as leading indicators inside a product team's span of influence.
- Fixed feature roadmaps communicate false certainty; outcomes preserve solution latitude.
- Outcomes as a two-way negotiation between product leadership and the product team.
- Learning goals before performance goals when the team does not yet understand what drives the metric.
- Avoid pursuing too many outcomes, changing outcomes too frequently, confusing outputs with outcomes, or optimizing one outcome without guardrails.
- Opportunity space tied to a desired outcome and framed as customer needs/pains/desires, not solutions.
- Opportunity structure as a way to decompose large strategic problems and deliver value over time.

Relevant sections reviewed:

- Chapter 3: Focusing on Outcomes Over Outputs.
- Chapters 6-7: Mapping and prioritizing the opportunity space.

## Jeff Patton - User Story Mapping

Primary contributions:

- Start with opportunities and make an early go-forward / trash decision before deep investment.
- Opportunity discussions focus on who, what, and why rather than implementation detail.
- Discovery should understand users, current behavior, intended change, solution behavior, and effort/risk.
- Prioritize outcomes rather than features.
- Release slices should be anchored in target outcomes and real-world user benefit.
- The minimum viable solution is the smallest solution release that can achieve its desired outcome, not the cheapest technically shippable subset.

Relevant sections reviewed:

- Outcome-focused slicing and release roadmap chapters.
- Chapter 11: Rock Breaking / Start with Opportunities.
- Discovery sections that connect opportunity, user context, and minimum viable solution.
