# Moderation and session protocol

Use this reference to keep sessions neutral, comfortable, and comparable.

## Setup

- Test the prototype, device, links, accounts, permissions, recording, audio, and screen sharing.
- Prepare backup links, data, and a plan for technical failure.
- Remove notifications and unrelated sensitive information.
- Give observers a separate channel so they do not interrupt.
- Pilot the complete session with someone outside the immediate design work.

## Session structure

A typical moderated session contains:

1. welcome, consent, and recording confirmation;
2. reassurance that the product is being tested;
3. brief relevant context questions;
4. think-aloud explanation;
5. first-impression or orientation prompt when useful;
6. realistic tasks;
7. neutral retrospective probes;
8. participant questions, thanks, incentive, and data reminder.

Allocate time from task complexity rather than copying a fixed schedule.

## Opening language

Communicate:

- difficulty is useful and not the participant's fault;
- honest reactions are welcome;
- the moderator may not answer product questions during a task;
- the participant can pause or stop;
- what is recorded and who will see it.

Do not claim anonymity when recordings or contact details make it impossible.

## Neutral prompts

Use sparingly:

- “What are you thinking?”
- “What are you looking for?”
- “What would you do next?”
- “What do you expect to happen?”
- “What does that mean to you?”
- “What would you do if I weren't here?”

Avoid:

- “Did you see the button?”
- “Wouldn't it be easier if…?”
- “Most people use this menu.”
- teaching terminology before it is tested;
- praise that signals the expected path.

## Assistance and stopping

- Let participants attempt and recover independently.
- Stop before distress, unsafe action, real data loss, or excessive unproductive time.
- If assistance is required, record the point, exact help, and resulting behavior.
- Distinguish prototype failure from comprehension or interaction failure.
- Never allow a study task to create an actual purchase, disclosure, deletion, or harmful commitment without explicit safe controls.

## Observation dimensions

Capture:

- interpretation of the goal;
- first action and path;
- visible hesitation and search;
- labels and controls interpreted differently than intended;
- errors, backtracking, and recovery;
- moments requiring memory or moderator help;
- success state and participant confidence;
- mismatch between claimed preference and observed behavior.

## Mobile and remote sessions

Let participants use their own device when environment and familiarity matter, while planning for privacy and capture limitations. In remote sessions, test connection and screen-sharing instructions, and distinguish network friction from product friction.
