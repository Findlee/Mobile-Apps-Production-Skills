# Observation and debrief

Use this reference to preserve raw evidence and decide what deserves action.

## Observation record

Capture one event per row:

```text
Session and task:
Timestamp or step:
Observed behavior:
Participant words:
Outcome or consequence:
Moderator assistance:
Initial interpretation:
Evidence confidence:
```

Keep observed behavior and quotes separate from interpretation. Do not “clean up” participant language into a claim they did not make.

## Observer behavior

- Watch the session rather than debating solutions live.
- Note surprising success as well as failure.
- Record the interface state and task context.
- Avoid diagnosing personality, intelligence, or motivation.
- After each session, independently select the most serious observed problems before group discussion.

## Immediate debrief

Debrief while sessions are fresh:

1. collect observed problems without discussion;
2. merge duplicate events by root cause only after preserving evidence;
3. identify the most consequential task failures;
4. distinguish prototype limitations, content problems, interaction problems, and system failures;
5. rank the issues the team can realistically address before the next round;
6. assign owners and a retest decision.

## Severity inputs

Consider:

- importance of the affected task;
- failure versus delay or annoyance;
- data, financial, privacy, safety, or trust consequence;
- ability to notice and recover;
- repeated occurrence within the tested sample;
- exposure across roles or contexts;
- confidence that the design caused the problem.

Do not convert counts from a small sample into population percentages.

## Keep separate lists

- serious observed usability problems;
- minor fixes that are genuinely quick;
- feature requests and preferences;
- research questions and conflicting evidence;
- technical or prototype defects;
- positive patterns to preserve.

This prevents easy work and speculative ideas from displacing important problems.

## Resist common reactions

- Do not add explanatory text before checking whether removing clutter or changing structure solves the cause.
- Do not implement a requested feature solely because a participant proposed it.
- Do not overreact to a momentary detour when the participant quickly recovers without harm.
- Do not dismiss a serious issue because a future redesign is planned.
- Do not treat observer consensus as stronger than session evidence.
